Waiting on a background process

Mockup for the issue. A session that ends its turn with a shell, a monitor, a background agent or a workflow still running, or with a wake-up scheduled, is not waiting on you. It says what it is waiting on, in blue and without the amber alert. Pick a case below to see every place the wording appears. The first card always shows a session that really needs you, for contrast.

The rules this mockup shows

  1. "Waiting for your input" and "Waiting for your approval" stay amber and count in "waiting on you". Nothing about them changes.
  2. A session idle on background work reads "Waiting for N background shell(s) to finish" (or monitor, agent, workflow), in blue with a ring, like Delegating. It keeps its place in the Waiting list, but is not amber and is not counted in "waiting on you".
  3. With several kinds, the text lists the counts: "Waiting for 1 background shell and 1 background agent to finish". The tooltip lists each task with a short description.
  4. A scheduled wake-up reads "Waiting for a scheduled wake-up at 14:30".
  5. The card says only the short line. It never carries a task description, so a long one cannot wrap it.
  6. The orchestrator callout adds the task description after the line, on its own row below it, cut at a character limit and ended with " ..." (a space, then three dots). The full text is in the tooltip.
  7. A shell's command is shortened and redacted before it leaves the machine, and is never sent whole.
  8. The blue ring pulses like the green running dot, on the same shared beat. It stops with reduced motion.
  9. In the activity feed the newest wait row is tinted and outlined like the card alert, with a kind pill (shell, agent, wake-up). It is blue with a pulsing ring for background work and amber with a plain ring for a wait on you. Once the wait is over the row goes plain.
  10. The moment the work ends, the wording goes back to normal ("Waiting for your input", or running when a new turn starts).
