Session recordingsession-recording

Lets a session's owner, or an Admin, record everything an orchestrator and its agents do, so a run can be replayed to see what happened and why. Needs sign-in on the server. Off by default.

On
A recording holds whole prompts, file contents, command output and tool results. Secrets are removed on the machine before it is sent, but that is best effort. A session is recorded only when its owner has allowed it in the reporter and someone has chosen it. Only the owner and an Admin can read a recording.

Session recording

How long recordings are kept and how much disk they may use.

Keep a recording for

Counted from when each part was recorded. Expiry runs every few minutes, so a short time is honored. The most you can set is 7 days.

What this mockup proposes

  • Admin. Session recording is a feature flag in Admin > Settings, beside the others, off by default, and unavailable when the server has no PODIUM_TOKEN. A section under it sets how long recordings are kept (hours or days, 6 hours by default, at most 7 days), a disk budget, and a limit for one session. A table lists every recording with its owner, who started it, its state, size and when it is deleted. An Admin can stop or delete any of them, each after a confirmation. Recordings also appear on #432's Stored activity page through its Source filter.
  • Choosing what to record. Record this session is in the orchestrator page's facts panel, in a new Recording cell, for its owner and for an Admin. The cell is a button like Resume and Fork while the session is not recorded; once it is recorded, the cell states the recording's state and when it goes, and the buttons for Stop, Open replay and Delete stay in the layer above the band. The button opens the app's modal with the Respond modal's option rows: the orchestrator, its agents and its forks, each with a box. Recording is per loop, starts when Start is pressed, and keeps nothing from before. The picker has five states: the owner allows it, after Start, the owner has not allowed it (nothing to press, and it names the sessionRecording option), the feature is off, and a viewer who is not the owner.
  • The notice cannot be hidden. Two always-on signs. On the wall, a recorded card gets a second band in the critical color with a filled dot and who started it (.band.recording). On the orchestrator page, a layer inside the page's surface above its band (.rec-bar) says what is kept, who started it, which loops are recorded and when it is deleted. Neither has an X or a switch. This is not a wall notice: those are for informational notes that someone may close, and consent is not one. The owner's terminal shows the same text (see the Owner's terminal screen).
  • Replay. A page of its own, reached from Open replay and from Admin's table, with a back link to the orchestrator page. It is the Activity feed's rows, oldest first, with the loop's name before each row, and a collapsed result under each tool call. A loop filter, a position slider and Jump to next failure move through it. A failed call uses the failure box. A compaction or a stop is a marked row. Show older pages back through a long run. It is read-only.
  • Getting into a replay after the recording has ended. Stopping a recording does not remove it: the page goes to a third state, "Recording stopped", with the same layer in the neutral color and a ring instead of the filled dot, and it keeps Open replay and Delete recording. The same layer shows on an ended session. An ended session is found in History: its row has a line under the name, "recording kept until 17:42", and opening the session shows its read-only page with that layer. Admin's Recordings table opens it too. Nothing is shown once the recording is deleted or has expired.
  • Thinking. The research on #353 found that the mod receives thinking without its text on the model and effort tested. The replay says so in the header, and each place the model thought has a Reasoning row that reads "Thinking: text is not available".
  • Confirmations. In Admin, the warning-bordered box with a button for each answer. Everywhere else, the Respond modal's confirmation box: a bold question, Cancel, and the critical-colored action button. Stop, Delete and Delete this recording all ask first and say what stays and what goes.
  • States. Replay has loading ("Loading…"), a failed load (the error box, with Try again), nothing recorded yet, a delete asking first, a stopped recording, a recording that reached its limit, and a viewer who is not the owner (the empty-state box with a plain refusal). A loop name too long for its row is cut with its full name as a title.

Source of truth

  • The markup is taken from the components on development, not from the standards pages, which follow main. That is why the facts panel has Machine and System, the card has the pin button (#496), and the replay's feed shows elapsed time as h:mm:ss, as the Activity feed does.

Reused as they are

  • Admin shell, nav, Settings rows and switch, the form panel, the table and the pager, the warning line and the warning-bordered box.
  • The session card and its bands, the orchestrator page with its facts, plan and agent tree, the modal and the Respond modal's option rows and confirmation box, the Activity feed rows, footer and older row, the failure box and its code block, the empty-state box, the error box and the back link.

New, proposed for the standards

  • .band.recording, .rec-bar (and its kept variant, .rec-bar.kept) and .badge.recording: nothing existing is a state that cannot be closed or switched off.
  • .feed li.mark and .feed li.cur: a marked row and the row the slider is on. Plus the loop's name (.who) and a collapsed result (.res) around the existing code block.
  • .fact.rec: the Recording cell, a thirteenth fact. The facts are twelve cells in two rows of six on development, so it takes a row of its own, full width. The alternative is to put it in the header or the band, which changes more of the page.
  • .scrub: a labelled range input. The standards have no slider.
  • The standards change only after you approve, through Standards: lines in the CHANGELOG, and the implementation adds each piece to the standards in its own PR.

Open decisions, with a recommendation

DecisionRecommendationWhy
1. Notice and the wall-notice rulesNot a wall notice. A band on the card, a layer on the page, no X, no switch.Consent cannot be dismissed.
2. Who gets RecordThe session's owner and an Admin, on the orchestrator page.Matches the read rule and the Respond model.
3. RetentionDefault 6 hours, at most 7 days.A recording exists to troubleshoot something now. The numbers can change before code.
4. Long runsThe replay loads the newest 200 rows, with Show older.The whole result is stored, but it should not all load at once.
5. Owner's consentA reporter option, sessionRecording, off by default.Same pattern as respond and activityFeed.
6. Replay address#/?open=<id>&replay, built on #542's address.Survives a reload, and a viewer who is not allowed sees the refusal at the same address.
7. Per-loop marks on the agent treeNot drawn. The layer names the recorded and unrecorded loops in words.A new column on the agent table is a bigger change to a shared component.

Not drawn

  • The capture, the batching, the load measurement and the disk budget's enforcement have no screen.
  • Modal focus and Escape follow accessibility.html and are not shown in a static page. The Admin menu collapses by the browser window's width, not the frame's.

Names, sessions, sizes and times are example data. This mockup is static: nothing is read from or written to a server.
